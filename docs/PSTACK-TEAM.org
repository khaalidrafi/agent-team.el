#+ROLE: how a team running agent-team.el (board, dispatches, chain) uses

        first dispatch references a playbook, principle, or skill.
#+LAW: pstack is vendored VERBATIM from github.com/cursor/plugins
        (Lauren Tan / poteto, MIT) at a pinned SHA in the

* What pstack is

pstack is poteto's rigorous-engineering skill stack. It is not one
skill; it is a router (=poteto-mode=) over 23 playbooks, 24 named
principles, and leaf skills for specific jobs (=unslop=, =tdd=,
=technical-writing=, =statechart=, =swarm=, =arena=, =interrogate=,
=benchmark-checklist=...). Its core claim fits this team's law
exactly: write less, higher quality code; verify before declaring
done; parallelize only what you can verify.

In the harness the tree lives read-only at
=/opt/agent-team-ci-skills/pstack/=. On the Lead desk it is the
local skills install. Both carry the same pin.

* Why a team wants it

- Playbooks standardize the shape of a work segment: an investigation
  segment ends with cited evidence, a bug-fix segment ends with a
  reproduction and a runtime-verified fix, a feature segment names
  its data shape first. The landing gate then checks real artifacts,
  not vibes.
- Named principles give the EM and members a shared vocabulary for
  course corrections. "Guard the context window" or "fix root causes"
  redirects a seat mid-task in two words, and the leaf file carries
  the full pattern.
- =unslop= enforces the house prose style on every surface the team
  emits: dispatches, segment reports, CHAT.org messages, commit
  messages, docs.

* The mapping to team law

| pstack concept | agent-team.el equivalent |
|----------------+--------------------------|
| Playbook steps | The DOD (definition-of-done) checkboxes of a board item |
| =show-me-your-work= decision trail | :LOGBOOK: notes + HANDOFF drawers |
| Fresh subagent, consolidated scope | A dispatch carries brief + directives + prior segment evidence |
| Verify against the real artifact | Landing gate: tests + checks in every session and at merge |
| Review by a non-author | Segregation law: author, verifier, merger are different models |
| /babysit, /shipping playbooks | The finalize job + the Lead's merge pass |

The two systems do not conflict: pstack disciplines the INSIDE of a
segment; the board disciplines the sequence of segments. A segment
report cites the principles it applied; the board cites the evidence
the segment produced.

* For the EM / Lead desk

- Route before you dispatch. When composing a DISPATCH, name the
  matching playbook from =skills/poteto-mode/SKILL.md= in the header
  (investigation, bug fix, feature, refactoring, prototype,
  session-pickup...). The member opens that playbook and copies its
  steps as their segment todos.
- Read the leaf before citing it. The law is absolute: cite only
  principles whose =skills/principle-*/SKILL.md= you actually read,
  and require the same of members. A principle cited unread is a
  false report.
- Use =session-pickup= on every Lead resume: read the prior trail
  (board, CHAT.org, STATE.org), reconstruct operational state, diff
  done vs pending, and do not re-derive what the trail already
  answers. The vault convo archive is the reduced timeline.
- Ask a subagent, not the Owner, when a fork is observable. If the
  answer is a fact you could learn by running something (behavior,
  timing, output), it is not the Owner's to answer. Sketch it with a
  throwaway prototype and let the result decide. Reserve :OWN:
  blockers for genuine preference or product calls.
- Own every delegated segment's result. Review the diff, write your
  own summary; never pass a member's self-report through as evidence.

* For member seats

- Open the named playbook first and copy its steps verbatim into your
  segment todos before any task-specific work. A skipped step stays
  in the list with =skip: <reason>=.
- Apply =unslop= to every prose surface you emit, reports and CHAT.org
  included. Short declarative sentences. No AI vocabulary, no em
  dashes, no colons as mid-sentence connectors. Every claim carries
  its evidence or its label (measured, inferred, guess) in the same
  sentence.
- Name the data shape first on any code task, per
  =principle-model-the-domain=. Choose the organizing structure
  before writing logic.
- Before reporting any measured number (speedup, token cost, file
  count), run the =benchmark-checklist= skill. Numbers without their
  limiting factor named are not evidence.
- Prove it works against the real artifact before flipping to REVIEW:
  run the feature, read the actual value, inspect the diff. "It
  compiles" and a green self-report are not proof.

* Standing integration notes

- The kit's global law (ci/AGENTS.md) carries the pstack routing law;
  every body reads it at bake time. No per-repo setup is needed
  beyond the submodule pin.
- Bumping the pin is a kit change, never a per-repo change: re-vendor
  from cursor/plugins, update the SHA comment and the counts in
  ci/AGENTS.md, land it in khaalidrafi/agent-team-ci, then bump the
  submodule pointer in consuming repos.
- The harness copy is read-only. A member that wants a variant
  playbook proposes it to the Lead; local forks of the pack inside
  work repos are compliance violations (INTEGRATION.md: port changes
  upstream first).
